Water damage in Evergreen, CO starts with one call

Water damage in Evergreen, CO usually begins small: a split supply line under a sink, a stained ceiling below a snow-loaded roof, creek water in a basement after a summer cloudburst. Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the water, drying the structure with measured equipment, cleaning what the water touched, and rebuilding what cannot be saved.

Evergreen Water Damage Pros runs a 24/7 emergency dispatch line for homes and small businesses in Evergreen and the nearby towns of Conifer, Indian Hills, Morrison, Golden and Idledale. The network sends licensed and insured restoration professionals, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Most calls are not dramatic. Someone notices a soft spot in a ceiling, a baseboard that has started to bubble, or a musty smell near a water heater. Those quiet leaks are the ones that grow into mold and framing damage, so the line is staffed for small questions as well as emergencies.

What water does in the Evergreen foothills

Evergreen sits at roughly 7,160 feet, and snow here averages about 80 inches a year. That climate drives a lot of local water damage. Roofs shed meltwater into soffits, pipes in unheated crawl spaces and vacation homes split during hard freezes, and thaw cycles push water along foundations. Each pattern points to a different fix, which is why water mitigation starts by finding the source rather than just running fans.

Bear Creek adds a second pattern. In June 2026 a night storm put as much as 6 inches of rain on parts of the area by the Sheriff's Office estimate, and Bear Creek rose from about 6 cubic feet per second to nearly 1,900. Mud and debris reached downtown Evergreen.

The Evergreen Metropolitan District issued a boil water order for specific areas after mains broke near El Pinal and Troublesome Creek. If you live along the creek, the practical lesson is to check your flood insurance before the next storm, because creek water is a different claim than a burst pipe.

The Mile High Flood District counts 24 known floods in the Bear Creek basin since the 1860s, and the 1896 flood that devastated Morrison was caused by rain centered on Cub Creek near Evergreen. Evergreen Lake is the drinking water supply for the Evergreen and Kittredge area, and the Evergreen Metropolitan District says the dam held up exceptionally well in the 2013 flooding.

The lake is not a flood control facility, so creek-side homes should not count on it to hold back a storm. For homes that took in creek water, flood damage restoration covers the contaminated-water work that a plumbing leak does not need.

How water category changes the job

Restoration pros sort water into three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. The category decides what is removed, what is cleaned, and what is safe to dry in place.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat usually happens
Category 1Supply line, broken pipe, rain through a roofExtract and dry. Materials often stay if treated quickly.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher overflow, sump pump failureExtract, clean and sanitize. Carpet pad and some porous materials are removed.
Category 3Creek flooding, sewage backup, long-standing waterRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize. Treat as contaminated.

Time matters because Category 1 water can degrade into Category 2 if it sits. Ask any company whether its crews work to S500, and ask how they document drying.

Mold is the quiet second problem

Mold needs moisture, so it follows water damage that was not dried quickly. EPA homeowner guidance is to dry wet materials within about one to two days and fix the water source first. Small surface growth on a limited area is often something a homeowner can handle with protection, while larger or hidden growth calls for a professional crew working to the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Our mold remediation service covers inspection, containment and removal after a leak. Health questions about mold belong to your physician, and the work here is about the building. If a buyer's lender has asked for mold clearance, ask about independent clearance testing, which is done by a separate party after remediation.

Insurance, explained without promises

Insurance wording varies, so read your own policy. In general, sudden and accidental water damage such as a burst pipe is often covered, while slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. Flood damage from creeks and storms is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Evergreen?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including weekends and holidays. Call, describe where the water is coming from, and you get shut-off and safety steps right away. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher explains what is possible for your address.

How is the price set, and when do I hear it?

Scope and price are explained before work starts. After a crew maps the moisture, you get the scope of work and how the cost is built, which depends on water category, affected area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope. We do not quote prices without seeing the loss.

Do you remove mold as well as water?

Yes, mold remediation is a secondary service. Many mold calls start with a past leak, so the crew finds the moisture source first and then contains and removes the growth. Ask whether any company you hire works to the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration dries and repairs what water damaged. Waterproofing changes how water reaches a foundation, and that is a different trade. A good restoration crew will tell you honestly when a basement needs drainage work afterward.

Can you help with a large insurance loss?

Yes. On larger losses the crew keeps photos, moisture logs and equipment records, and you decide what to send your insurer. Call your insurer early, and ask what documentation it wants before repairs start.

Do you rent out air movers and dehumidifiers?

Drying equipment is placed and monitored by the crew, because equipment sitting in a room does not prove the structure is dry. Describe your situation to dispatch and ask what is possible.
