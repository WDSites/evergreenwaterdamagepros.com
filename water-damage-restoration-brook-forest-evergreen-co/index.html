Water Damage Restoration in Brook Forest, Evergreen, CO

Brook Forest homes sit higher than most of Evergreen, so freezing weather arrives sooner and part-time cabins can leak for days unnoticed. Here is what to check, who to call and how restoration works on a mountain property.

Brook Forest is high country, and the plumbing knows it

According to a place-name directory entry on Wikipedia, Brook Forest is a census-designated place and unincorporated community along Brook Forest Road, split between Jefferson and Clear Creek counties, with a 2020 population of 622. It lists the elevation at about 8,449 feet. Mail uses the Evergreen ZIP, 80439.

That is well above the roughly 7,160 feet of Evergreen itself, and the practical result is an earlier freeze. A cabin that sits empty through a cold snap can have a supply line split behind a wall, and the water only shows up when someone opens the door. See frozen pipe water damage for how those losses are handled.

What we can say about local flood history

No documented flood or storm event names Brook Forest in the sources checked for this page. The 2013, 2014 and 2026 events seen elsewhere in the Evergreen area are not attributed to it here.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. The causes to plan for are ordinary ones: a failed water heater, a line that froze, a roof that let in snow melt.

Whose water is it, the district's or yours

The Brook Forest Water District says on its own site that it formed in 1964 near the historic Brook Forest Inn. It serves about 295 homes and 2 commercial buildings from three deep groundwater wells, with storage tanks and more than 7 miles of pipe. According to Evergreen Metropolitan District, its own service boundary reaches south as far as Brook Forest Road, so Brook Forest water is not EMD water.

The consequence is simple. A main problem or a loss of pressure in the street is the district's to fix, while a leak inside your house is yours. If water is pouring in, shut the supply at your own valve when it is safe to reach and call the district about anything on its side.

Sewer service is not documented for this page, so ask which sewer or septic system serves your address. According to Jefferson County Public Health, septic in unincorporated Jefferson County falls under its oversight, so a backup that involves a septic tank is a conversation with that office. For a backup, sewage backup cleanup covers the contaminated-water side.

Mountain homes, seasonal cabins and long driveways

Part-time cabins fail quietly. A water heater that weeps for a week, a toilet supply line or a roof edge that sheds snow melt into the wall can soak insulation before anyone notices. A water heater leak is one of the easier causes to stop fast, and a vacant house makes it harder.

A home on a well adds a second problem. A leaking house can keep the pump running, and a pressure tank that cycles constantly may wear out or fail. Shutting the supply early protects the pump as well as the floors, and a plumber or well contractor should check the pump and tank after the leak is found.

Older mountain houses often have a crawl space where the first water collects. The crawl space water damage page explains moisture readings there, and water mitigation explains the drying that follows. When you call, ask the dispatcher about access to your property, since long or steep driveways affect how equipment is carried in.

Landmarks, so you know you are in the right place

The Brook Forest Inn, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, anchors the community's history. The Lower Maxwell Falls trailhead, run by the US Forest Service, is reached from Highway 73 south of Evergreen and Brook Forest Road. Both are named only so you can tell the dispatch line which side of the hills you are on.

The Evergreen Fire Protection District's wildfire plan treats Brook Forest as one of its planning units. Smoke and fire damage are outside this service, so call your insurer and a fire-damage specialist for that.

Safety, documentation and insurance

Do not wade into standing water, and do not touch breakers while standing in it. Skip household fans and ordinary vacuums for sewage or other contaminated water. Photograph the damage when it is safe, and call your utility or 911 if there is immediate danger. Restoration professionals follow ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Moisture readings, photos and an itemized scope help document a loss. Check your policy, since coverage depends on cause and terms. The Colorado Division of Insurance advises contacting your insurer early and sending the inventory in one package.

If mold shows up after a leak, see mold remediation, ask whether the provider offers it and ask whether your state licenses mold work.

For the county side, ask which building office covers your address. Jefferson County Building Safety is at 303-271-8260 for the Jefferson side, and the office can tell you what applies.

Frequently asked questions

Is Brook Forest part of Evergreen?

It uses the Evergreen ZIP, 80439, but it is its own unincorporated community on Brook Forest Road, and it spans two counties. Ask which county's building office covers your address before any repair work.

Can I call about water damage in Brook Forest after hours?

Yes, you can call the dispatch line at any hour. The dispatcher handles urgent calls and confirms what is possible for your address, including access.

Who do I call if the water main or pressure fails?

The Brook Forest Water District runs the wells, tanks and pipe in its system, so a main problem or pressure loss is theirs. A leak inside the house is yours to stop and to dry.

Why do cabins here freeze sooner than homes in town?

At about 8,449 feet, Brook Forest sits well above the roughly 7,160 feet of Evergreen, so cold hits earlier. Part-time homes should have the water shut off and drained or kept heated.

Does a leak affect my well pump?

It can. A leaking house may keep the pump and pressure tank cycling, so close the supply early and have the equipment checked afterward.
