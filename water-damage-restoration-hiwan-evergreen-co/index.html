What water damage looks like around the Hiwan golf course

According to a place-name directory, Hiwan Hills is a named place inside the Evergreen census-designated place. A local Evergreen guide describes Hiwan Hills and Hiwan as built around the Hiwan Golf Club, with homes ranging from A-frame chalets to sprawling mid-century ranches. That mix means no single failure pattern fits every house, so the first job is finding where the water entered.

The same guide describes The Ridge at Hiwan as newer homes, late 1990s onward, on large lots near Elk Meadow Park, which is Jefferson County Open Space. If you are unsure how old your supply lines are, ask a plumber before a problem shows up.

Where the water usually starts

Each source leaves a different pattern, and a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera show how far the water traveled.

Water heater

A failed tank or fitting soaks the floor around it and wicks into nearby walls. See water heater leak cleanup.

Appliances

Dishwasher, washer and refrigerator lines leak behind and under units, often unseen until flooring lifts. See appliance leak damage.

Frozen pipes

Lines in unheated or empty spaces can split in a cold snap. See frozen pipe water damage.

Snow and roof leaks

Melting snow and ice dams push water under shingles and into ceilings. See roof leak water damage.

Why Evergreen weather matters here

NOAA station summaries put Evergreen at about 7,160 feet, with roughly 80 inches of snow and roughly 18.6 inches of precipitation a year. A thaw after that much snow can send meltwater behind flashing or into attic insulation, and pipes in unheated spaces face freeze risk.

In a vacant or part-time home, a split line can run for days before anyone sees it. Ask a plumber about heat settings and a shut-off plan, and have someone check inside.

Water and sewer service

Evergreen Metropolitan District, the primary source here, names Hiwan and The Ridge as communities inside its water and wastewater service area. For the areas it names, that makes the district your utility, not a private well. Ask which utility serves your address before you call anyone about a supply problem.

First steps while you wait

Water damage safety for Hiwan homeowners

  • Shut off the water only if you can reach the valve safely. If the leak is on the utility side of the meter, call the utility instead.
  • Do not touch breakers or outlets while standing in water. If water is near electrical panels, stay back and call the utility or 911 if there is immediate danger.
  • Do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on sewage or contaminated water.
  • Photograph the damage when it is safe, before you move anything.
  • Keep people and pets out of rooms with sagging ceilings.

How the work usually goes

Restoration professionals extract standing water, map moisture with meters and thermal cameras, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry wet materials. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any provider how its crews apply it. For wet framing or subfloor, structural drying is a separate step that needs readings, not guesses.

Insurance and mold

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help document the loss. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms, so check your policy. The Colorado Division of Insurance advises contacting your insurer as soon as possible, keeping receipts if you cannot stay home, and sending an inventory of damaged property in one package, since information sent in pieces slows processing.

If wet materials stay damp, mold can follow. Ask whether your provider offers mold remediation and whether your state licenses mold work.
