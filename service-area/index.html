Kittredge, ZIP 80457, sits on Bear Creek between Evergreen and Idledale and is served from the same dispatch line. In June 2026 Bear Creek overflowed there, with water lines of 2 to 3 feet on some homes. If you live there, the Morrison and Idledale pages describe the creek-flood side of the work.

How local conditions change the work

Mountain homes see freeze damage and snowmelt, as in Conifer, where the elevation is higher than Evergreen and many properties rely on wells and septic systems. Creek-side communities see sudden flooding. City neighborhoods such as Golden have municipal water and sewer, so the first call may include the city utility.

Indian Hills is a mixed case. Part of the community is served by the Indian Hills Water District, and other homes use wells and septic, so a water loss there can involve a supply issue as well as a house issue. See the Indian Hills page for details.

What to expect when you call from outside Evergreen

Dispatch asks for your address, the source of the water and what is affected. Road and weather conditions can change how quickly a crew can reach a canyon or mountain address, and the dispatcher tells you what to expect without promising an arrival time. While you wait, the page for your town lists steps to take safely.

Mention-only places that sometimes come up include Lakewood, Genesee and Red Rocks Park. Genesee uses the Golden post office, and Red Rocks Park sits near Morrison. If your address is nearby and not listed, call and ask. Dispatch can confirm whether it is within the service area.

Septic systems in unincorporated Jefferson County are regulated by Jefferson County Public Health, and private well owners are responsible for their own water testing. If a flood reached a well or a septic tank, get both checked before you resume normal use. Jefferson County Building Safety handles permits in unincorporated areas, and Golden and Morrison have their own building offices.

What to have ready when you call

Dispatch works faster when you can answer a few questions. You do not need perfect information, and a rough answer is fine.

  • The street address and the nearest cross road or landmark.
  • Where the water is coming from and whether it has been stopped.
  • Which rooms and floors are wet and about how long it has been wet.
  • Whether the water looks clean, cloudy or smells like sewage.
  • Whether electrical outlets or the panel are near the water.
  • Your insurer's name, if you have it, for your own claim records.
