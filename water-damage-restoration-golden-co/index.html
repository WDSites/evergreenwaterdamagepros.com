Water damage across Golden, block by block

Golden runs along Clear Creek between North and South Table Mountain, and the water risk changes with every turn of the map. Downtown sits beside the creek, the Washington Avenue blocks hold older masonry and wood-frame buildings, the Colorado School of Mines campus on Illinois Street fills with aging plumbing and student rentals, and the gulch neighborhoods climb toward Lookout Mountain and Golden Gate Canyon.

A burst line, a failed water heater or a backed-up sewer looks different in each of them.

Near the creek and in lower downtown, groundwater and storm sewers decide how basements behave after hard rain, so a damp slab can mean a seepage problem rather than a leak. On the slopes, water arrives faster and leaves through walls, crawl spaces and window wells. We bring moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to trace where it went before anything gets torn out.

When water is spreading in Golden

Call right away if water is moving across a floor, dripping through a ceiling or rising in a basement. Wet drywall, subfloor and insulation start to fail quickly, and the sooner emergency water removal starts, the more of the structure stays dry. We dispatch around the clock for 80401, which holds downtown, the Mines campus and the main city core, and for 80403.

Find the shutoff

Turn the home's water off at the meter pit or the main valve. If the leak seems to come from the city's side of the meter, call the City of Golden utilities emergency line.

Watch the electrics

Stay away from outlets, cords and appliances that sit in or near water, and keep everyone out of wet basements.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, shoes, books and small furniture to dry ground. Leave heavy pieces alone so you do not strain yourself.

Photograph first

Take photos and short video of the water and the damage before moving anything, then contact your insurer.

Safety first: electrical, gas and sewage

  • Do not step into standing water if outlets, cords, a furnace or a water heater might be live. Cut power at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water. If not, call 911 or your electric utility first.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or light anything.
  • Sewage backing up from a floor drain is contaminated. Stay out, keep children and pets away, and report a possible city sewer problem to the City of Golden utilities emergency line.

What Tucker Gulch and Kenneys Run tell Golden homeowners

Mile High Flood District records show Golden has been flooded by creeks and dry gulches for a long time. On July 23 and 24, 1965, about 4.5 inches of rain fell in an hour on Tucker Gulch and water spread across roughly 17 blocks, reaching 69 homes. The gulch was channelized afterward, but a lined channel still depends on clear culverts and clean inlets.

The May 6, 1973 event was closer to a basement story. Kenneys Run spilled out, plugged storm sewers pushed water down Ford and Jackson Streets, and basements flooded there.

If you live on an older Golden street, check your sump pump and its discharge line, clear leaves from window wells, and have a battery backup before the next storm season. For pooling that already happened, flood damage restoration covers extraction, drying and sanitizing, and standard flood losses usually need separate flood coverage, so check your policy.

City water, city sewer and the Building Division

Golden is a city-utility town. The City of Golden supplies drinking water drawn from Clear Creek and treated at its own plant, and it runs the sewer system too. Its Utilities Division answers water and sewer emergencies around the clock, so a main break in the street or a sewer backup that clears nothing inside the house belongs on the City of Golden utilities emergency line.

That differs from the mountain communities around Evergreen, where wells and septic systems are common. In Golden, a failed supply line on your side of the meter is a plumbing repair, but trouble on the city side is the city's to fix.

Repairs also pass through the City of Golden Building Division, where plumbing, mechanical, electrical and water heater permits are handled over the counter. Ask the Division what your repair needs, and we will schedule our part of water damage repair around it.

Neighbors we mention only for orientation include Lakewood, Genesee and Red Rocks Park. They sit near Golden but they are not what this page covers. If you are closer to the canyon, see our pages for Morrison or Indian Hills, where creek floods and wells shape the work differently.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water removal available in Golden?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7 for Golden, and crews carry extraction units, air movers and moisture meters. Same-day response is offered when available.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Golden?

We serve 80401, the core around downtown, City Hall, the School of Mines and Genesee mail, and 80403. Who handles a water main break in Golden?

In Golden the city owns the water and sewer system, so a break in the street or a sewer main problem goes to the City of Golden utilities emergency line. Trouble inside the home, past the meter, is a private plumbing and drying job.

Will my basement flood again if I dry it out?

Drying removes the current water but not the cause. Golden has a history of basement flooding from gulches and plugged storm sewers, so ask a plumber about sump pump capacity, check the discharge line, and keep window wells clear. We can test materials with moisture meters to confirm they are dry.
