Why Morrison floods the way it does

Records kept by the Mile High Flood District (UDFCD) count 24 known floods in the Bear Creek basin since the 1860s. The flood of record at Morrison came on July 24, 1896, when rain centered on Cub Creek near Evergreen sent nearly 3 feet of water down Main Street, with a peak of about 8,600 cfs. Practical point: a cloudburst miles up the canyon can reach town while the sky over your street is still clear.

Later events show the pattern. A cloudburst near Idledale on July 7, 1933 put about 8,110 cfs through Morrison, and the September 2 and 3, 1938 flood did roughly $450,000 in damage. In September 2013, Bear Creek reached about 3,200 cfs in town and Morrison was under an evacuation alert for several days. If you live in the canyon corridor, keep a go-bag by the door and watch weather alerts during summer storms.

The Jefferson County Hazard Mitigation Plan (2021) puts 60 buildings, about 32% of the town, in the 1% annual chance floodplain. Take two steps this week: verify your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and read your policy. Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners coverage and often needs separate flood insurance, so ask your insurer what yours includes.

Before we arrive, if water is still spreading

Stay out of creek water

Floodwater and creek debris are contaminated. Do not wade through it and never drive through flooded roads, including stretches of Colorado Highway 8.

Lift what you can

Raise small valuables, papers and electronics to dry shelves or upper floors, but only if you can do it without stepping into standing water.

Stop the source

If the water comes from a broken pipe or fixture, close the valve that feeds it. Creek or storm water cannot be stopped, so move yourself first.

Photograph before cleanup

Take wide and close photos of the water line and affected rooms. Keep receipts, and contact your insurer before making repairs.

Floodwater, power and gas

  • Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  • Cut power at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. If not, call the utility or 911 first.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not light anything or flip switches.
  • Do not enter a flooded basement. Water hides steps, debris and live wiring.

Historic buildings, older systems

The Morrison Historic District holds about 70 buildings and sites along Colorado Highway 8 and was listed on the National Register on September 28, 1976. Many are stone, brick or old timber construction, and Morrison sandstone was also quarried for Denver landmarks. Practical point: dense masonry and old plaster hold moisture longer than modern drywall, so drying takes patience and monitoring, and a thermal camera plus moisture meters should guide when drying is finished rather than a calendar.

In older houses in the 80465 area, ask a plumber about the age of supply lines. For a slow leak or a failed line, our water damage cleanup work covers removal of wet materials, cleaning and drying. If you are not sure whether damage is sudden or gradual, tell your insurer exactly what you saw, because sudden and accidental water damage is often covered and gradual leaks often are not.

Town utilities and permits

The Town of Morrison runs its own water treatment plant and wastewater treatment plant through its Public Works Department. If a main breaks or sewage backs up from the street, call the Town at Town Hall, 321 Colorado Highway 8, because that is the Town's system and not your home's plumbing. Homes in unincorporated areas outside town limits may fall under Jefferson County instead, and septic systems there are regulated by Jefferson County Public Health.

For permits, the Town of Morrison Building Department covers commercial and multi-family structures, so ask the Town which office handles a single-family home. Outside town limits, ask Jefferson County Building Safety. We do not decide permit requirements, so confirm with the right office before repairs begin.
