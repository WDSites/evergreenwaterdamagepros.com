Kittredge is its own community, ZIP 80457

Kittredge is not an Evergreen neighborhood. According to a place-name directory (Wikipedia), it is a separate census-designated place in Jefferson County with its own ZIP, 80457, a 2020 population of 1,308 and an elevation of about 7,074 feet, strung along Bear Creek on Colorado Highway 74. Upstream is Evergreen, and downstream the road runs to Idledale.

Some local web pages label Kittredge places as "Evergreen, CO 80439." That label can differ from your real mailing address, so tell the dispatcher your street address and ask them to confirm it before anything is scheduled. See the full service area list for the wider region.

What Bear Creek did in June 2026

The June 24 to 25, 2026 flash flood is the event to know. The Canyon Courier reported Bear Creek crested its banks in several places at about 1,890 cubic feet per second, up from about 6 before the storm according to the sheriff's office. Rainfall reports varied, with an official figure near 3.5 inches and unofficial reports up to about 6.

Denver7 described water lines of 2 to 3 feet on homes along Avenue F, and a backyard on Avenue D flooded with water partway up a garage cabinet and debris piled in the yard. If your garage, crawl space or ground floor took water, assume the wet material reaches higher and deeper than the visible line.

Denver7 also reported that patios, fencing and furniture were swept away, a camper and two trailers reportedly floated off, and a feeder creek near the old Country Cafe overflowed the road. Kittredge Park, run by the Evergreen Park and Recreation District, took the biggest hit and closed. Debris carried onto a property can hide damage, so walk it only when the water is gone and photograph before you move anything.

A creek that has done this before

The Mile High Flood District's Bear Creek history records an August 9, 1934 cloudburst near Kittredge that killed six people, and 24 known floods on the creek since the 1860s. Most were summer thunderstorm floods that rise and fall in hours. The practical lesson is speed: water can be in a garage and gone again before you have a chance to react, leaving mud and wet framing behind.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. Kittredge Sanitation and Water District serves the community, and according to Evergreen Metropolitan District, EMD operates its water and wastewater, so call them if you suspect a utility problem rather than a house problem.

Mud, silt and creek water are contaminated-water work

Overbank creek water carries silt, soil and whatever it swept up on the way, so this is a flood damage restoration job rather than a pipe leak. Porous materials that sat in it, such as carpet, pad and insulation, are usually removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized. Water damage cleanup covers the debris, odor and contents side once the standing water is out.

When water is still present, emergency water removal uses extraction units to pull it out of garages and ground floors. Crawl spaces trap water out of sight, so ask about crawl space water damage if the space under the house was reached.

Drying and checking before you rebuild

Wet framing, subfloor and wall cavities should be measured, not guessed at. Restoration professionals use moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to map the wet areas, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers and keep logging readings until materials are dry. Structural drying is the step that decides whether you rebuild on dry framing. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard for water damage work, and you can ask any provider whether its crews follow it.

Mold can follow a wet structure that stays damp. Ask whether the provider offers mold remediation and whether the state licenses mold work.

Safety before you go back in

  • Do not wade into floodwater or mud-laden water, and keep children and pets out.
  • Do not run breakers or touch outlets while standing in water. If the panel is wet or unreachable, call your electric utility or 911.
  • Do not use household fans or an ordinary vacuum on sewage or contaminated water.
  • Shut off water only if you can reach the valve safely. Call 911 or the utility for any immediate danger.
  • When it is safe, photograph the water line, damaged contents and debris before cleanup.

Insurance: flood is a separate policy

The Colorado Division of Insurance advises contacting your insurer early and sending an inventory in one package. Flood coverage is separate from homeowners coverage, so check your policy to see what applies to creek overflow. Coverage depends on the cause and the terms, and nothing here predicts an outcome. Photos taken before cleanup, moisture readings and an itemized scope all help document the loss.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kittredge part of Evergreen?

No. Kittredge is a separate census-designated place in Jefferson County with ZIP 80457. Some web pages label it Evergreen, CO 80439, so give the dispatcher your street address and ask them to confirm it.

Can I call about water damage in Kittredge after hours?

You can call the dispatch line at any hour. Dispatch handles urgent calls and confirms what is possible for your address, so describe where the water is and whether anyone is in danger.

Is cleaning up creek mud the same as fixing a leak?

No. Silt and creek water are contaminated, so the work involves removing affected materials, sanitizing and drying, which is the flood damage restoration approach. A pipe leak is usually cleaner water and a simpler job.

Does homeowners insurance cover Bear Creek flooding?

Flood damage is usually handled by separate flood coverage, so check your policy and ask your insurer. Photograph everything before cleanup and send your inventory in one package, as the Colorado Division of Insurance advises.

What should I do first if the creek has come into my garage?

Stay out of the water and keep power off if the panel is wet. Call for help, photograph the water line when safe, and ask about extraction and drying before mud sets into the framing.
