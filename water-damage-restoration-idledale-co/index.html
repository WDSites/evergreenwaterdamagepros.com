Getting to a Bear Creek Canyon home

Idledale sits on CO-74 in Bear Creek Canyon, about 6,600 feet up, and that highway is the way in. Many homes here stand close to the creek, so a storm upstream can put water in a crawl space or basement quickly. Because the canyon road is the main route, we ask callers about road conditions first, and our dispatcher explains what is possible once we know where the water is and how the highway looks.

What Bear Creek has done before

Bear Creek is a flash-flood stream. The Mile High Flood District counts 24 known floods in the basin since the 1860s, and two of them centered on Idledale. On July 7, 1933, a cloudburst near town sent water down Sawmill Gulch and Vernon Creek and ruined the highway between Morrison and Idledale. On August 24, 1946, heavy rain made Bear Creek overflow near Idledale. After the 1938 flood, federal crews built retaining walls along the creek.

The practical lesson is speed. Most basin floods come from summer thunderstorms, and the water rises and falls within hours, which leaves mud and silt behind long after the creek has dropped. If your property was wet, the cleanup clock starts when the water leaves, not when the rain stops.

In June 2026 a Wednesday-night storm sent Bear Creek from about 6 cubic feet per second to nearly 1,900, according to the Jefferson County Sheriff's Office. Upstream in Kittredge, water lines of 2 to 3 feet marked home exteriors, and CO-74 closed for a few hours. Idledale lies downstream of that stretch, so neighbors here know how fast a quiet creek can change.

What creek water does inside a house

Water from Bear Creek is not like a burst supply line. It is classed as contaminated, so porous materials such as carpet pad, insulation and soaked drywall usually have to be removed, not dried in place. Mud settles into floor cavities, behind baseboards and under sheet flooring, and it holds moisture long after the surface looks clean.

Our crews work with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find wet framing and subfloor before opening walls. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry what is kept, while HEPA air scrubbers handle dust during demolition. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Insurance and permits

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs separate flood coverage, so check your policy and ask your insurer before you start repairs. Keep photos and receipts, and send your insurer information in one complete package, because sending it in pieces slows processing.

Idledale is unincorporated, so rebuilding questions go to Jefferson County Building Safety at 303-271-8260. Ask them whether your repair needs a permit. Septic systems in unincorporated Jefferson County are regulated by Jefferson County Public Health, so ask them if floodwater reached a septic area.

Safety before restoration begins

  • Floodwater can hide live electrical hazards. Stay out of wet rooms if outlets, cords or panels are submerged, and have power shut off by a qualified person.
  • If you smell natural gas or propane, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not turn lights or switches on or off.
  • Propane tanks can shift or float in high water. Keep away from displaced tanks and report them.
  • Do not run generators indoors or in a garage, and do not touch wet appliances.

What should I do about mud and silt left by Bear Creek?

Do not leave it to dry in place. Silt traps moisture against floors and framing, so it should be shoveled out or extracted, and the area then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Wear boots, gloves and a mask, and keep children and pets away from it.

Will my homeowners policy cover creek flooding?

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs separate flood coverage. Sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe is often covered. Check your policy and ask your insurer about limits and exclusions.
