Where Bergen Park sits, and why it matters when water shows up

Bergen Park is a named community in the federal place-name database (GNIS), listed at roughly 7,790 feet according to a place-name directory. The Evergreen weather station sits at about 7,160 feet, so treat town forecasts as a guide and give any pipe in an unheated corner attention before the first hard freeze, not after.

The name also belongs to a historic Denver Mountain Park, owned by the City and County of Denver, established in 1915 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. If your problem is at a park address, that is a landowner question. A water loss in a house or a shop is a separate matter and the one this page covers.

A mixed area: homes, shops and offices

Jefferson County held a 2015 community-plan meeting on the "El Rancho and Bergen Park Activity Centers," according to a county planning topic. That tells us the area mixes homes with commercial activity along Bergen Parkway. We will not guess at the proportion, and you do not need it to start a call.

What matters is who is calling. A homeowner with a wet kitchen and a tenant with water under a shop counter need different questions answered, because the contents, the lease and the insurance differ. Tell the dispatcher which one you are, and the conversation starts in the right place. Small-business owners can read how we handle commercial water damage before they phone.

Winter loads: snow, frozen lines and empty rooms

Evergreen-area climate figures show roughly 80 inches of snow a year, using the Evergreen station as the reference. Snow that melts on a warm afternoon and refreezes at dusk can push water under roofing edges and into ceilings, which is where roof leak water damage usually begins. Clear snow from low roof edges and watch ceilings after each thaw.

Storage rooms, back offices and vacant units are the quiet risk. Nobody walks in daily, so a frozen supply line can split and run for days before anyone sees it. Keep a little heat on in rooms with plumbing and know where the shutoff is. If a line has already burst, frozen pipe water damage explains what the cleanup involves.

Leaks that start indoors

Water heaters and appliances fail in houses and in shop back rooms alike. A tank that weeps at the base, a dishwasher hose, a utility sink supply or a small breakroom refrigerator line can soak flooring before it shows on a wall. Check the floor around these items now and then. For the usual causes and the drying steps, see water heater leak cleanup.

Once the source is stopped, water mitigation is the stage where extraction units remove standing water, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the space, and moisture meters show when materials are back to normal. Restoration professionals commonly reference ANSI/IICRC S500, a published standard for water damage work, and you can ask any provider whether it follows it.

What the record says about flooding here

No documented flood or storm history names Bergen Park. Coverage of the September 2013 flood and the June 2026 flood did not name it, so this page makes no claim about flood risk here. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so check your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.

Most water losses in a place like this start inside the building: a failed fitting, a frozen line, a melting roof edge. That is why the checklist above is about interiors.

Water and sewer outside the building

Evergreen Metropolitan District names Bergen Park as a community in its service area. According to the district's who-we-are page, the former Bergen Park water and sanitation district became part of West Jefferson County Metropolitan District, which the Evergreen Metropolitan District operates.

The consequence is practical. If water is bubbling up outside, a main seems to be leaking, or sewage backs up and clears nothing inside, the problem may belong to the district. Ask the district which side of the meter the failure is on before paying for a repair. Sewage is contaminated water, and sewage backup cleanup is handled differently from a clean supply leak.

Safety first: water, power and sewage

  • Do not wade into standing water, and never touch breakers or switches while standing in it. If the panel is not dry and reachable, call 911 or your electric utility.
  • Shut off the water only if you can reach the valve safely. If you smell gas or see immediate danger, leave and call 911.
  • Do not use household fans or ordinary vacuums on sewage or contaminated water. Keep children and pets out.
  • Photograph the damage when it is safe, before moving anything.

Insurance: home policy or commercial policy

A homeowner policy and a commercial policy or lease can treat the same burst pipe differently. Check your own policy or lease, because coverage depends on the cause and the terms. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope all help document the loss.

The Colorado Division of Insurance advises contacting your insurer early and sending your inventory in one package. Nothing here promises approval or direct billing, and a shop tenant should also ask the landlord who insures the building.

Mold after water

If materials stay wet, mold can follow, and mold remediation is the page to read. Ask whether the provider offers it and whether the state licenses mold work. Health questions belong with a physician.
