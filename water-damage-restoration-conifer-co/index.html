Why winter and thaw drive Conifer water losses

Conifer sits at roughly 8,000 feet plus, higher than Evergreen, so freezes arrive earlier and linger longer. A supply line in an unheated crawl space or a vacant cabin can split during a cold snap and then run unseen for hours once temperatures climb. The damage usually shows up as a stained ceiling or a soft floor, not a gushing pipe.

Snow adds a second route for water. Heavy loads on a roof melt unevenly, ice builds at the eaves, and meltwater backs up under shingles and drips into insulation. We trace those slow leaks with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras before opening a single wall, so you pay for the demolition the loss actually requires.

Spring thaw tests foundations and window wells next. If a basement in 80433 takes in meltwater, our crews start with emergency water removal using extraction units, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to pull the remaining moisture out of framing and subfloor.

Before we arrive, if water is still spreading

Stop the source

Find the shutoff where water enters the house, which on a well system is usually near the pressure tank. Turn it off before you do anything else.

Power only if safe and dry

Switch off breakers for wet areas only if the panel is in a dry spot and you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise leave it alone.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, electronics, paperwork and photos to a dry, higher spot. Leave heavy furniture where it stands.

Keep people and pets clear

Standing water may be contaminated, especially if septic or a sump line backed up. Keep children and animals out, then call the line.

Shut down the well pump

If a pipe has failed downstream of your pressure tank, turn off the pump breaker, but only if that panel is dry. A running pump keeps feeding the leak from the well.

Electrical, well pump and gas risk

  • If water has reached outlets, the panel or a pump control box, stay out of that area and do not touch switches.
  • Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. If you are unsure, do not go near it.
  • If you smell gas or see sparking, leave the house and call 911 or your utility before you call us.
  • Never run a submersible or pressure-tank pump that has been under water until a qualified contractor has checked it.

Wells, pumps and septic after a loss

The Jefferson County Conifer/285 Corridor Area Plan describes water here as coming from wells on individual parcels or a central system, and it prefers five-acre minimum lots for a well plus onsite septic. In practice, most Conifer homes have a pump, pressure tank and treatment tank that a city house never has. A pressure tank leak or a pump that cycles nonstop can flood a mechanical room, so we check that equipment when we trace a source.

Septic adds another question. Septic systems in unincorporated Jefferson County are regulated by Jefferson County Public Health, and a backup into a basement is a contaminated-water loss, not a clean one. If a wet area might involve sewage, ask us about flood damage restoration and about cleaning up to ANSI/IICRC S500 practices. Well owners should also ask Jefferson County Public Health about testing after any flooding, since Colorado does not regulate private well water quality.

The US-285 corridor, place by place

Homes strung along US-285 face different problems than those on side roads. Around Aspen Park, the Village at Conifer shopping center runs its own water and wastewater under the Aspen Park Metropolitan District, but homes nearby rely on private wells. If your house is in that area, assume your own pump and tank are your responsibility when water starts moving.

Farther west, Pine Junction and the Shaffers Crossing turnoff toward Staunton State Park mean longer approaches on mountain roads. When you call, tell us your access: driveway length, gate codes, any steep or unpaved stretch, so the crew can plan its equipment. The Jeffco plan also names the Tiny Town floodplain, and it calls for "Climb to Safety" signs where roads can flood. If your street has such signs, keep your address handy for the dispatcher.

Older homes near the Meyers Ranch area, where the Midway House dates to 1889, can have mixed plumbing from several remodel eras. That mix is a reason to let a technician inspect adjacent walls instead of drying only the visible stain.

What we do for Conifer homes

Most calls start with water mitigation to stop damage from spreading: extraction, controlled demolition of wet drywall and insulation, then drying with air movers and dehumidifiers. Once everything reads dry, water damage repair brings back drywall, flooring and finishes.

Moisture that stays trapped in a mountain home's cold wall cavity invites growth. If you see staining or a musty smell after a leak, mold remediation done to ANSI/IICRC S520 is the next step to ask about, and you should ask whether your state licenses mold work. EPA homeowner guidance says to dry wet materials within about one to two days and fix the water source first.

We also serve neighbors in Indian Hills, where steep terrain and mixed well and district water create similar concerns, and homes in Morrison down the creek. Wildfire hazard is high across much of the Conifer area. We handle water damage and the drying that follows, but smoke and soot cleanup is outside our service.

Insurance varies. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer before repairs.

Will a pipe freeze and burst at this elevation even if the heat is on?

It can. Cold air can reach lines in crawl spaces, exterior walls, garages and vacant homes while the thermostat reads normal.

After a freeze loss, call early, because a slow leak behind a wall may continue for a long time before you see it.
